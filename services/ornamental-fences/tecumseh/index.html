Ornamental Fences for Tecumseh homes

Tecumseh stretches from lakeside neighbourhoods on Lake St. Clair to the Oldcastle industrial area and farmland in the former Sandwich South township. Ornamental fencing works well for Tecumseh homes on larger lots and near the water, where an open design lets wind pass straight through. On the rural side of town, we also use it to frame front yards and driveway entrances on properties that use farm fence elsewhere.

What's included

Aluminum and steel ornamental fences with clean pickets and finials, for front yards, pools and properties that want security without hiding the view. Depending on your property, the work can include:

  • Aluminum or steel
  • Styles and tops
  • Pool-ready spacing
  • Racked on slopes
  • Matching gates
  • Posts in concrete
  • Site visit.
  • Written quote.
  • Posts and panels.
  • Gates and finish.

Full details, how the work runs and common questions are on our main ornamental fences page. Want a ballpark first? Try the instant fence estimate.

Local conditions in Tecumseh

Wind off Lake St. Clair puts extra load on solid fences in north Tecumseh, so we tighten post spacing or suggest designs that let some air through. In the rural south, longer runs and open ground call for heavier terminal posts and bracing.

Questions to ask before you hire

Whoever you hire for ornamental fences in Tecumseh, get clear answers to these before signing:

  • Is the material aluminum or steel, and residential or commercial grade?
  • Will the panels be racked or stepped on my slope?
  • Does the design meet pool enclosure rules if I need it to?
  • What finish warranty does the manufacturer offer?

By-laws, permits and locates

Tecumseh asks homeowners to get a fence permit, make sure the fence is on their own property and book a final inspection. Fences can be up to 1.9 metres in rear and side yards and 1.22 metres in front yards. Pool fences must be at least four feet high, not climbable and fitted with spring-loaded, self-latching gates on the pool side. Rules change, so we confirm current requirements with the Town and request utility locates before digging.
