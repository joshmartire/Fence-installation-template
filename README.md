# Fence Website Template

A reusable, production-ready static website for fence contractors and fence installation companies, built to the Rank Boss master website specification (technical SEO, local SEO, schema, accessibility, legal drafts).

The centrepiece is a **pinned scroll sequence that builds every fence type from the ground up**: as the visitor scrolls, post holes are dug, posts drop in, concrete footings pour below the frost line, rails slide in, then pickets, panels, mesh or boards go on and the caps pop on, before the finished fence sinks away and the next one starts. Wood, vinyl, ornamental aluminum, chain link and composite, one after another.

It also includes an **instant fence estimate**: material, height, length (with a "measure my yard" helper), ground, gates, old fence removal and extras, priced from the client's own per-foot rates.

Static HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No frameworks, no tracking and no third-party requests at all: fonts are self-hosted and every drawing is inline SVG generated by the build.

Demo: `rankboss-fence.pages.dev` (noindexed). Same engine as the HVAC and roofing templates, so every tweak from the other sites carries over.

**Never hand-edit the generated HTML.** Change `_build/`, then rebuild.

## Setting up a new client

1. Edit `_build/site.config.json`: business name, legal name, domain, phone, email, hours, address (or `null` for a service-area business), Google Business Profile, social profiles.
2. Brand and offer:
   - `services`: delete any slug the client doesn't offer. Its pages, menu and footer links, sitemap entries, form options, **its fence in the scroll sequence** and **its estimate material** disappear. Fix any sentences listed in `_build/removed-service-mentions.txt` (QA fails while it exists).
   - `accent` (main brand colour for buttons and highlights) and `accent_alt` (the steel second colour), both `#rrggbb`. The build stops if either is too dark to read.
   - `logo`: client logo in `assets/img/client/` (transparent PNG best).
   - `photos`: the client's own job photos only (see `_build/photos.py`).
   - `locate_service` / `locate_url`: Ontario One Call in Ontario; 811 for US clients.
3. **Estimate pricing** (`estimator` block), the client's real numbers:
   - `materials[].per_ft`: typical installed price per linear foot at 6 ft on flat ground, including posts in concrete, rails, infill, standard hardware and clean-up. `heights` = heights offered for that material. `walk_gate` / `double_gate` = installed price of one gate. Remove a material the client doesn't install.
   - `heights[].mult`, `terrain[].mult`, `removal_per_ft` (0 hides removal), `addons[]` (`per_ft` or `flat`, limited by `materials`, hidden with `service`), `length` slider range, `range_pct`, `minimum_job`, `round_to`.
   - `enabled: false` removes the tool, its nav links, hero picker and legal paragraphs.
4. `form_endpoint`: FormSubmit (`https://formsubmit.co/CLIENT-EMAIL`). The first submission sends an activation email that must be confirmed.
5. Cities changed? Rewrite `content_cities.py` (including the by-law paragraph) and each service's `"cities"` text. Keep it genuinely local.
6. Review everything marked `CLIENT REVIEW` (HTML comments and config notes): hero badges, working standards, about page, by-law details, legal drafts.
7. Build and test:
   ```bash
   pip install pillow fonttools brotli playwright
   python3 _build/build.py
   python3 _build/qa.py
   python3 _build/qa_browser.py --axe path/to/axe.min.js   # axe optional: npm pack axe-core
   ```
8. At launch: set `"domain"` to the client's domain and `"demo_mode": false` (removes noindex and the yellow demo banner).

## The scroll sequence: how it works

The section is (number of fence types + 1) screens tall and pins the drawing in place. Each fence type gets one screen of scrolling. `main.js` turns the scroll position into a build progress `--b` (0–1) on the current fence card and nothing else; every part of the drawing carries its own start and duration (`--s`, `--d`) on that 0–1 timeline, and CSS works out its motion: holes scale down into the clay, dirt piles appear, posts drop in with an ease-out and a puff of dust, concrete fills the holes from the bottom, rails extend from post to post, pickets/panels/boards drop or slide in one after another (chain link mesh is woven across as a roll travels the line), then caps and trim pop on and a "BUILT" stamp lands. At the end of its slot, the fence sinks away and the next one starts digging. A readout shows the type (01/05), the current step ("Pouring footings") and the build percentage, with a six-step progress strip underneath.

Timeline: dig 0.02–0.20 · posts 0.12–0.30 · concrete 0.26–0.40 · rails 0.36–0.52 · infill 0.48–0.82 · caps 0.82–0.90 · built 0.88.

On phones it's the same build, stacked under the heading with one fence description at a time. With reduced motion or without JavaScript, there's no pinning: all five fences are shown fully built in a grid with their names. To add a sixth fence type, add it to `TYPES` in `fence_art.py` with a drawing function and an entry in `pages.py` → `fence_types`.

## Opening animation: "the gates"

A full-screen, about 3-second build: two hinge posts and a latch post slam into the ground with screen shake, dust and sparks; the rails and Z-braces of two gates snap across; charred pickets rain down from the centre outward while amber light bleeds through the gaps; the business name is branded into the wood with a flash and a final hit; then both gates swing open toward the viewer and the page appears in the light behind them. It's pure CSS and HTML (timings in `main.css`, OPENING ANIMATION), so it starts on first paint with nothing to download.

It plays on **whichever page a visitor opens first** in a browser session (home page or a service page from Google), then never replays that visit. It's skipped for a `#hash`, for reduced-motion users and on the 404 and thank-you pages, and a tap or key press throws the gates open immediately. Headlines are in the HTML underneath from the first paint, so it never hides content from Google. `"intro_scope": "home"` limits it to the home page; `"intro": false` turns it off.

## How the instant estimate works

`(per_ft × height mult × terrain mult + removal/ft + per-foot extras) × length + gates + flat extras` → ± `range_pct`, rounded, never below `minimum_job`. Heights are limited to those each material comes in, extras only show for the materials they apply to, and the result card shows a live drawing of the chosen fence at the chosen height. "Measure my yard" adds up width and depth for the sides ticked (back, left, right, front). Everything runs in the browser; nothing is sent until the visitor submits the lead form, which carries material, height, length, gates, ground, extras and the range shown. The home page's material picker is a plain form, so it works without JavaScript and deep-links (`/fence-estimate/?material=vinyl`).

## Design

Warm charcoal with a timber-amber accent and a steel second colour (both from config). Anton headlines, Barlow body, JetBrains Mono for readouts. Blueprint-style dimensions, cut-away ground with clay hatching and a frost line, a perspective fence line at golden hour in the hero, a yard plan that measures its own fence line, and a lot plan with colour-coded utility locate flags. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. No giant business name in the footer.

## Deploying

Cloudflare Pages (like the other Rank Boss demos): connect the repo, no build command, output directory `/`. `_headers` noindexes the `*.pages.dev` hostname only. For GitHub Pages: Settings → Pages → `main` / root; the build writes `CNAME` once a real domain is set.

## Adding a page later

- New service: add an entry to `content_services.py` (with its three `cities` paragraphs), a `COSTS` and `ASK` entry in `content_costs.py`, add the slug to `services` in the config, rebuild, run QA. Menus, footer, sitemap, schema and city pages update automatically.
- New city: add an entry to `content_cities.py` and a paragraph for it in every service's `cities`, rebuild, run QA.
